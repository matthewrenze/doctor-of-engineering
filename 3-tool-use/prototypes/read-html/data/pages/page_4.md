**Philosophy** ('love of wisdom' in [Ancient Greek](/wiki/Ancient_Greek
"Ancient Greek")) is a systematic study of general and fundamental questions
concerning topics like [existence](/wiki/Existence "Existence"),
[reason](/wiki/Reason "Reason"), [knowledge](/wiki/Knowledge "Knowledge"),
[value](/wiki/Value_\(ethics_and_social_sciences\) "Value \(ethics and social
sciences\)"), [mind](/wiki/Mind "Mind"), and [language](/wiki/Language
"Language"). It is a rational and critical inquiry that reflects on its
methods and assumptions.

Historically, many of the individual [sciences](/wiki/Science "Science"), such
as [physics](/wiki/Physics "Physics") and [psychology](/wiki/Psychology
"Psychology"), formed part of philosophy. However, they are considered
separate academic disciplines in the modern sense of the term. Influential
traditions in the [history of philosophy](/wiki/History_of_philosophy "History
of philosophy") include [Western](/wiki/Western_philosophy "Western
philosophy"), [Arabic–Persian](/wiki/Islamic_philosophy "Islamic philosophy"),
[Indian](/wiki/Indian_philosophy "Indian philosophy"), and [Chinese
philosophy](/wiki/Chinese_philosophy "Chinese philosophy"). Western philosophy
originated in [Ancient Greece](/wiki/Ancient_Greece "Ancient Greece") and
covers a wide area of philosophical subfields. A central topic in
Arabic–Persian philosophy is the relation between reason and
[revelation](/wiki/Revelation "Revelation"). Indian philosophy combines the
[spiritual](/wiki/Spirituality "Spirituality") problem of how to reach
[enlightenment](/wiki/Enlightenment_in_Buddhism "Enlightenment in Buddhism")
with the exploration of the nature of reality and the ways of arriving at
knowledge. Chinese philosophy focuses principally on practical issues about
right social conduct, government, and [self-cultivation](/wiki/Self-
cultivation "Self-cultivation").

Major branches of philosophy are [epistemology](/wiki/Epistemology
"Epistemology"), [ethics](/wiki/Ethics "Ethics"), [logic](/wiki/Logic
"Logic"), and [metaphysics](/wiki/Metaphysics "Metaphysics"). Epistemology
studies what knowledge is and how to acquire it. Ethics investigates moral
principles and what constitutes right conduct. Logic is the study of [correct
reasoning](/wiki/Logical_reasoning "Logical reasoning") and explores how good
[arguments](/wiki/Argument "Argument") can be distinguished from bad ones.
Metaphysics examines the most general features of [reality](/wiki/Reality
"Reality"), existence, [objects](/wiki/Object_\(philosophy\) "Object
\(philosophy\)"), and [properties](/wiki/Property_\(philosophy\) "Property
\(philosophy\)"). Other subfields are [aesthetics](/wiki/Aesthetics
"Aesthetics"), [philosophy of language](/wiki/Philosophy_of_language
"Philosophy of language"), [philosophy of mind](/wiki/Philosophy_of_mind
"Philosophy of mind"), [philosophy of religion](/wiki/Philosophy_of_religion
"Philosophy of religion"), [philosophy of science](/wiki/Philosophy_of_science
"Philosophy of science"), [philosophy of
mathematics](/wiki/Philosophy_of_mathematics "Philosophy of mathematics"),
[philosophy of history](/wiki/Philosophy_of_history "Philosophy of history"),
and [political philosophy](/wiki/Political_philosophy "Political philosophy").
Within each branch, there are competing [schools of
philosophy](/wiki/Schools_of_philosophy "Schools of philosophy") that promote
different principles, theories, or methods.

Philosophers use a great variety of methods to arrive at philosophical
knowledge. They include [conceptual analysis](/wiki/Conceptual_analysis
"Conceptual analysis"), reliance on [common sense](/wiki/Common_sense "Common
sense") and [intuitions](/wiki/Intuition "Intuition"), use of [thought
experiments](/wiki/Thought_experiments "Thought experiments"), analysis of
[ordinary language](/wiki/Ordinary_language "Ordinary language"), [description
of experience](/wiki/Phenomenology
