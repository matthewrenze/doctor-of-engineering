## History

Main article: [History of philosophy](/wiki/History_of_philosophy "History of
philosophy")

As a discipline, the history of philosophy aims to provide a systematic and
chronological exposition of philosophical concepts and doctrines.[38] Some
theorists see it as a part of [intellectual
history](/wiki/Intellectual_history "Intellectual history"), but it also
investigates questions not covered by intellectual history such as whether the
theories of past philosophers are true and have remained philosophically
relevant.[39] The history of philosophy is primarily concerned with theories
based on rational inquiry and argumentation; some historians understand it in
a looser sense that includes [myths](/wiki/Myth "Myth"), [religious
teachings](/wiki/Religion "Religion"), and proverbial lore.[40]

Influential traditions in the history of philosophy include
[Western](/wiki/Western_philosophy "Western philosophy"),
[Arabic–Persian](/wiki/Islamic_philosophy "Islamic philosophy"),
[Indian](/wiki/Indian_philosophy "Indian philosophy"), and [Chinese
philosophy](/wiki/Chinese_philosophy "Chinese philosophy"). Other
philosophical traditions are [Japanese philosophy](/wiki/Japanese_philosophy
"Japanese philosophy"), [Latin American
philosophy](/wiki/Latin_American_philosophy "Latin American philosophy"), and
[African philosophy](/wiki/African_philosophy "African philosophy").[41]

### Western

Main article: [Western philosophy](/wiki/Western_philosophy "Western
philosophy")

[![Bust of
Aristotle](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/Aristotle_Altemps_Inv8575.jpg/250px-
Aristotle_Altemps_Inv8575.jpg)](/wiki/File:Aristotle_Altemps_Inv8575.jpg)[Aristotle](/wiki/Aristotle
"Aristotle") was a major figure in ancient philosophy and developed a
comprehensive system of thought including metaphysics, logic, ethics,
politics, and natural science.[42]

Western philosophy originated in [Ancient Greece](/wiki/Ancient_Greece
"Ancient Greece") in the 6th century BCE with the [pre-Socratics](/wiki/Pre-
Socratics "Pre-Socratics"). They attempted to provide rational explanations of
the [cosmos](/wiki/Cosmos "Cosmos") as a whole.[43] The philosophy following
them was shaped by [Socrates](/wiki/Socrates "Socrates") (469–399 BCE),
[Plato](/wiki/Plato "Plato") (427–347 BCE), and [Aristotle](/wiki/Aristotle
"Aristotle") (384–322 BCE). They expanded the range of topics to questions
like [how people should act](/wiki/Ethics "Ethics"), [how to arrive at
knowledge](/wiki/Epistemology "Epistemology"), and what the [nature of
reality](/wiki/Metaphysics "Metaphysics") and [mind](/wiki/Mind "Mind")
is.[44] The later part of the ancient period was marked by the emergence of
philosophical movements, for example, [Epicureanism](/wiki/Epicureanism
"Epicureanism"), [Stoicism](/wiki/Stoicism "Stoicism"),
[Skepticism](/wiki/Skepticism "Skepticism"), and
[Neoplatonism](/wiki/Neoplatonism "Neoplatonism").[45] The medieval period
started in the 5th century CE. Its focus was on religious topics and many
thinkers used ancient philosophy to explain and further elaborate [Christian
doctrines](/wiki/Christian_doctrine "Christian doctrine").[46][47]

The [Renaissance](/wiki/Renaissance "Renaissance") period started in the 14th
century and saw a renewed interest in schools of ancient philosophy, in
particular [Platonism](/wiki/Platonism "Platonism").
[Humanism](/wiki/Renaissance_humanism "Renaissance humanism") also emerged in
this period.[48] The modern period started in the 17th century. One of its
central concerns was how philosophical and scientific knowledge are created.
Specific importance was given to the [role of reason](/wiki/Rationalism
"Rationalism") and [sensory experience](/wiki/Empiricism "Empiricism").[49]
Many of these innovations were used in the [Enlightenment
movement](/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment "Age of Enlightenment") to challenge
traditional authorities.[50] Several attempts to develop comprehensive systems
of philosophy were made in the 19th century, for instance, by [German
idealism](/wiki/German_idealism "German idealism") and [Marxism](/wiki/Marxism
"Marxism").[51] Influential developments in 20th-century philosophy were the
emergence and application of [formal logic](/wiki/Formal_logic "Formal
logic"), the focus on the [role of language](/wiki/Linguistic_turn "Linguistic
turn") as well as [pragmatism](/wiki/Pragmatism "Pragmatism"), and movements
in [continental philosophy](/wiki/Continental_philosophy "Continental
philosophy") like phenomenology, [existentialism](/wiki/Existentialism
"Existentialism"), and [post-structuralism](/wiki/Post-structuralism "Post-
structuralism").[52] The 20th century saw a rapid expansion of academic
philosophy in terms of the number of philosophical publications and
philosophers working at [academic institutions](/wiki/Academic_institution
"Academic institution").[53] There was also a noticeable growth in the number
of [female philosophers](/wiki/Female_philosophers "Female philosophers"), but
they still remained underrepresented.[54]

### Arabic–Persian

Main articles: [Islamic philosophy](/wiki/Islamic_philosophy "Islamic
philosophy") and [Iranian philosophy](/wiki/Iranian_philosophy "Iranian
philosophy")

[![Portrait of Avicenna on a Silver
Vase](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Avicenna_Portrait_on_Silver_Vase_-
_Museum_at_BuAli_Sina_%28Avicenna%29_Mausoleum_-_Hamadan_-
_Western_Iran_%287423560860%29.jpg/250px-Avicenna_Portrait_on_Silver_Vase_-
_Museum_at_BuAli_Sina_%28Avicenna%29_Mausoleum_-_Hamadan_-
_Western_Iran_%287423560860%29.jpg)](/wiki/File:Avicenna_Portrait_on_Silver_Vase_-
_Museum_at_BuAli_Sina_\(Avicenna\)_Mausoleum_-_Hamadan_-
_Western_Iran_\(7423560860\).jpg)Portrait of [Avicenna](/wiki/Avicenna
"Avicenna"), one of the most influential philosophers of the [Islamic Golden
Age](/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age "Islamic Golden Age")

Arabic–Persian philosophy arose in the early 9th century CE as a response to
discussions in the [Islamic theological tradition](/wiki/Kalam "Kalam"). Its
classical period lasted until the 12th century CE and was strongly influenced
by ancient Greek philosophers. It employed their ideas to elaborate and
interpret the teachings of the [Quran](/wiki/Quran "Quran").[55]

[Al-Kindi](/wiki/Al-Kindi "Al-Kindi") (801–873 CE) is usually regarded as the
first philosopher of this tradition. He translated and interpreted many works
of Aristotle and Neoplatonists in his attempt to show that there is a harmony
between [reason](/wiki/Reason "Reason") and [faith](/wiki/Faith "Faith").[56]
[Avicenna](/wiki/Avicenna "Avicenna") (980–1037 CE) also followed this goal
and developed a comprehensive philosophical system to provide a rational
understanding of reality encompassing science, religion, and mysticism.[57]
[Al-Ghazali](/wiki/Al-Ghazali "Al-Ghazali") (1058–1111 CE) was a strong critic
of the idea that reason can arrive at a true understanding of reality and God.
He formulated a detailed [critique of
philosophy](/wiki/The_Incoherence_of_the_Philosophers "The Incoherence of the
Philosophers") and tried to assign philosophy a more limited place besides the
teachings of the Quran and mystical insight.[58] Following Al-Ghazali and the
end of the classical period, the influence of philosophical inquiry waned.[59]
[Mulla Sadra](/wiki/Mulla_Sadra "Mulla Sadra") (1571–1636 CE) is often
regarded as one of the most influential philosophers of the subsequent
period.[60] The increasing influence of Western thought and institutions in
the 19th and 20th centuries gave rise to the intellectual movement of [Islamic
modernism](/wiki/Islamic_modernism "Islamic modernism"), which aims to
understand the relation between traditional Islamic beliefs and modernity.[61]

### Indian

Main article: [Indian philosophy](/wiki/Indian_philosophy "Indian philosophy")

[![Painting of Adi
Shankara](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/Raja_Ravi_Varma_-
_Sankaracharya_-_cropped.png/250px-Raja_Ravi_Varma_-_Sankaracharya_-
_cropped.png)](/wiki/File:Raja_Ravi_Varma_-_Sankaracharya_-_cropped.png)[Adi
Shankara](/wiki/Adi_Shankara "Adi Shankara") developed the
[monistic](/wiki/Monism "Monism") view of [Advaita
Vedanta](/wiki/Advaita_Vedanta "Advaita Vedanta"), stating that the existence
of a plurality of distinct entities is an [illusion](/wiki/Maya_\(religion\)
"Maya \(religion\)").

One of the distinguishing features of Indian philosophy is that it integrates
the exploration of the nature of reality, the ways of arriving at knowledge,
and the [spiritual](/wiki/Spirituality "Spirituality") question of how to
reach [enlightenment](/wiki/Enlightenment_in_Buddhism "Enlightenment in
Buddhism").[62] It started around 900 BCE when the [Vedas](/wiki/Vedas
"Vedas") were written. They are the foundational scriptures of
[Hinduism](/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism") and contemplate issues concerning the
relation between the [self](/wiki/%C4%80tman_\(Hinduism\) "Ātman
\(Hinduism\)") and [ultimate reality](/wiki/Brahman "Brahman") as well as the
question of how [souls](/wiki/Jiva "Jiva") are reborn based on their [past
actions](/wiki/Karma "Karma").[63] This period also saw the emergence of non-
Vedic teachings, like [Buddhism](/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism") and
[Jainism](/wiki/Jainism "Jainism").[64] Buddhism was founded by [Gautama
Siddhartha](/wiki/Gautama_Siddhartha "Gautama Siddhartha") (563–483 BCE), who
challenged the Vedic idea of a [permanent self](/wiki/Anicca "Anicca") and
proposed [a path](/wiki/Noble_Eightfold_Path "Noble Eightfold Path") to
liberate oneself from [suffering](/wiki/Du%E1%B8%A5kha "Duḥkha").[64] Jainism
was founded by [Mahavira](/wiki/Mahavira "Mahavira") (599–527 BCE), who
emphasized [non-violence](/wiki/Ahi%E1%B9%83s%C4%81 "Ahiṃsā") as well as
respect toward all forms of life.[65]
